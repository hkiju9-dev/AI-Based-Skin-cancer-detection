Understanding Skin Cancer: Causes, Risks, and Prevention

Skin cancer encompasses various conditions affecting skin cells, with risk factors like UV exposure, fair skin, family history, and weakened immunity. Early detection through regular skin checks and tools like AI prediction models can significantly improve survival rates and treatment outcomes.

Skin Cancer Types

Types of Skin Cancer

Skin cancer is the abnormal growth of skin cells, most often caused by excessive sun exposure. It is one of the most common cancers worldwide, but when detected early, it is highly treatable. Below are the most common types of skin cancer.

Melanoma
Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)
Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)
Merkel Cell Carcinoma

Melanoma

The most dangerous form of skin cancer, melanoma develops in the cells that give skin its color. It can spread rapidly to other organs if not caught early. Early detection through AI-assisted image analysis is critical for survival.

Basal Cell Carcinoma

The most common type of skin cancer, BCC begins in the basal cells of the skin and usually appears as a slightly transparent bump on sun-exposed skin. It rarely spreads but can cause local tissue damage if untreated.

Squamous Cell Carcinoma

SCC occurs in the flat cells that form the outer layer of the skin. It often appears as a firm, red nodule or flat sore with a scaly crust and is more likely to spread than BCC if not treated promptly.

Merkel Cell Carcinoma

A rare but aggressive form of skin cancer that appears as flesh-colored or bluish-red nodules, often on the face, head, or neck. It tends to grow quickly and has a higher risk of spreading to other parts of the body.

AI Prediction Graphs

Precision, model loss, and accuracy are key metrics for evaluating a model's performance.

Precision

Precision refers to the proportion of positive predictions that were actually correct.

Loss and Accuracy

'Loss' measures prediction error, while 'accuracy' measures correct predictions.

Model Loss

Model Loss represents the error or difference between the predicted values and the actual values.

Model Accuracy

Accuracy is the percentage of correct predictions made by the model out of all predictions.
